Orbits, Escape and Satellites

An orbit is a fall that never lands. Fire a ball sideways fast enough and the ground curves away beneath it as fast as it falls. A little faster and it swings out on an ellipse; faster still and it never comes back. This page follows that one idea through orbital speed, escape speed, the energy of a satellite and why astronauts float.

The law of gravitation, g and potential energy −GMm/r are built on the previous page: Gravitation.

1 · Newton’s cannon

A cannon on a very tall mountain fires horizontally, with no air in the way. Predict what each speed does, then fire. vorb is the speed for a circular orbit just above the ground.

Circular orbit: gravity is exactly the centripetal force, GMm/r² = mv²/r, so vorb = √(GM/r). Near the surface GM/R² = g, so vorb = √(gR) ≈ 7.9 km s⁻¹. Slower, and the ball curves in faster than the ground falls away: it lands. Faster, and it climbs on an ellipse, with the launch point as its nearest point. At √2 vorb the ellipse never closes: that is the escape speed.

2 · Escape speed: climbing out of the energy well

Fire straight up. The ball trades kinetic energy for potential energy as it climbs the curve U = −GMm/r. Its total energy E is a flat line. Where the line meets the curve, K is zero and it turns back. Raise the speed until the line reaches zero: then there is no turning point, and it escapes.

potential energy U(r)total energy Ekinetic energy = E − U

To reach infinity with zero speed left, E = ½mv² − GMm/R must be at least 0. So ve = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR) = √2 · vorb, about 11.2 km s⁻¹ for the Earth. The mass m cancels, and so does the direction: energy does not care which way you fire, as long as the path does not hit the planet (and we ignore air and the Earth’s spin). From a height h, use R + h in place of R.

simplified The Moon’s escape speed is only about 2.4 km s⁻¹. Over billions of years, the fastest gas molecules near its surface leak away, which is one reason it has almost no atmosphere.

3 · Higher orbits are slower, and much longer

For a circular orbit of radius r = R + h: vorb = √(GM/r) and T = 2πr/vorb = 2π√(r³/GM). Go higher and the speed drops, while the path gets longer, so the period grows fast: T² ∝ r³, Kepler’s third law again. Slide the height (the height scale is logarithmic).

4 · Two useful orbits: geostationary and polar

Pick the height so that T is exactly one rotation of the Earth, and put the orbit over the equator, going the same way the Earth turns. The satellite then hangs over one spot, so a dish can point at it and never move. Seen from above the North Pole:

5 · The energy of an orbiting satellite

A satellite in a circular orbit has K = ½mvorb² = GMm/2r and U = −GMm/r. So K is exactly half the size of U, and the total is E = −GMm/2r: negative. Slide the orbit outward and watch the three bars.

K = GMm/2rU = −GMm/rtotal E = −GMm/2r

Why negative? U is zero at infinity, and K can never be negative. If E were zero or positive, the satellite would have enough energy to reach infinity: it would escape (section 2). A bound orbit, circle or ellipse, always has E < 0. For an ellipse, E = −GMm/2a, where a is the semi-major axis.

The orbit paradox. Moving to a higher orbit needs energy (E rises towards 0), yet the satellite ends up slower (K = −E falls). The extra energy, and more, goes into U. The reverse is true too: air drag takes energy away, the orbit shrinks, and the satellite speeds up. To escape from an orbit you must supply the binding energy GMm/2r, which means raising the speed from vorb to √2 vorb.

6 · Weightless, not gravity-less

A bathroom scale does not measure gravity. It measures how hard it has to push up on you. Put the scale in a lift and change how the lift accelerates.

Newton’s second law for you: mg − N = ma (down positive). The scale reads N = m(g − a). In free fall a = g, so N = 0. An orbiting spacecraft and the astronaut inside are both in free fall round the Earth, with the same acceleration, so nothing presses on anything: weightlessness. At 400 km up, gravity is still about 89% of its surface value. It is not missing; it is what keeps them in orbit.
