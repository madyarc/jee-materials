Rolling Motion

A rolling wheel does two things at once: its centre moves forward at v, and it spins at ω. Rolling without slipping is the special case where the two match, v = ωR, so the bottom point is momentarily at rest. Everything on this page (who wins a race down a ramp, which way friction acts, how a skidding ball starts to roll) follows from comparing v with ωR.

1 · Every point of the wheel

Blue arrows are the velocities of points on the wheel, measured from the ground. Set the centre’s speed v and the rim’s spinning speed ωR separately, or pick a named case.

Every arrow is the centre’s v plus the spin’s ωR along the rim. On the ground, the bottom point moves at v − ωR. That one number, the slip velocity, tells you whether the wheel rolls (0), skids (> 0) or spins its wheels (< 0), and kinetic friction always points against it.

Write I = βMR², where β = k²/R² is a pure number fixed by the shape: ring 1, hollow sphere 2/3, disc 1/2, solid sphere 2/5. Rolling down, a = g sin θ / (1 + β). Mass and radius cancel; only β matters, and the smallest β wins. Friction points up the slope with f = β/(1 + β) · Mg sin θ, so rolling needs µ ≥ β/(1 + β) · tan θ.

Lower µ below a body’s bar and it slips: kinetic friction µMg cos θ is then too weak to spin it up in step, a = g(sin θ − µ cos θ) is larger than when rolling, and the extra energy is lost to friction instead. The grey block slides with no friction at all (β = 0 in effect): nothing can beat it.

3 · Slipping turns into rolling

Throw a ball along a rough floor without spin, or spin a disc and set it down. At first v ≠ ωR, so the contact point slides and kinetic friction acts against that sliding. It changes v and ωR in opposite directions until they meet. From then on the ball rolls and friction drops to zero.

Strobe: the ball every equal time step. Hollow = slipping, filled = rolling.

Shortcut for the final speed: friction acts along the floor, so it has no turning effect about any point on the floor, and angular momentum about such a point stays fixed: Mv₀R + βMR²ω₀ = MvR + βMR²(v/R). So vfinal = (v₀ + βω₀R)/(1 + β), whatever µ is. µ only decides how long it takes.

4 · Where to push so no friction is needed

A horizontal force F acts on a rolling body at height h above its centre (a string, a cue, a hand). Slide the height and watch the friction the floor must supply to keep it rolling.

Take torques about the contact point (friction and the normal force pass through it): F(R + h) = IPα with IP = MR²(1 + β). Then Ma = F + f gives f = F(h/R − β)/(1 + β). It vanishes at h = βR = k²/R. For a billiard ball that is 2R/5 above the centre: struck there, it rolls at once without skidding.
