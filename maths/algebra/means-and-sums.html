Means and sums you can see

Three averages drawn inside one semicircle, sums of powers arranged as blocks, and telescoping sums laid out on a number line. Each picture shows why its formula is true. The formula is a caption to read once the picture makes sense.

1 · AM ≥ GM ≥ HM on one semicircle

Lay a and b end to end and draw the semicircle on the whole length. Drag the split point P. The radius is the AM. The half-chord standing at P is the GM. Drop a perpendicular from P onto the radius to cut off the HM.

2 · Sums of powers as blocks

Each sum is a shape. The formula is what you get by counting the shape a second way.

3 · Telescoping: the middle cancels

Some terms are the gap between two neighbouring ticks on a number line. Put n such gaps end to end and only the two outer ticks matter. Every tick in the middle is the end of one gap and the start of the next, so it cancels.

4 · Recurrences an = p·an−1 + q beyond NCERT

There is one value L that the rule leaves unchanged: L = pL + q, so L = q/(1 − p). Measure every term from L. Each step multiplies that distance by p, so the distances form a GP. That is the whole method.

Based on NCERT Mathematics Class 11, "Sequences and Series", and Hall & Knight, Higher Algebra. The pictures are classical "proofs without words" (R. B. Nelsen, Proofs Without Words). Each picture shows particular numbers; the captions state the general results, which hold for every n and, for AM ≥ GM ≥ HM, for any number of positive values.
