Identities as Pictures

Four pictures carry most of the identities in this chapter: two right triangles stacked on each other (compound angles), two arrows added tip to tail (a sin x + b cos x), two waves added (sum-to-product), and a chain of equal arrows (sums of angles in AP). Drag, slide, and read the formula off what you see.

1 · sin(A + B): stack one triangle on another

Turn by A, then by B. The radius OP (length 1) ends at angle A + B, so its height is sin(A + B) and its shadow is cos(A + B). The right triangle OQP (legs cos B and sin B) is tilted by A, and each leg splits into a vertical and a horizontal piece.

Drag near Q to change A, near P to change B.

What the picture says

Why the angle at P is A: PQ is perpendicular to OQ and PS is perpendicular to the x-axis, so the angle between PQ and PS equals the angle between OQ and the x-axis.

picture covers acute A, B only The formulas hold for all real A, B; NCERT proves the general case with the distance formula on the unit circle. Putting −B for B gives sin(A − B) and cos(A − B).

2 · a sin x + b cos x is one arrow beyond NCERT

Draw an arrow of length a at angle x. Its height is a sin x. Add an arrow of length b at angle x + 90°: its height is b sin(x + 90°) = b cos x. The two arrows are perpendicular, so tip to tail they make one arrow of length R = √(a² + b²), turned ahead by a fixed angle φ. Its height is R sin(x + φ), and a height can never exceed the arrow's length.

3 · Sum to product: two waves make a wave inside an envelope

Add sin Cx and sin Dx. Where the two waves are in step they reinforce; where they are out of step they cancel. The result is a fast wave at the average rate (C + D)/2, squeezed by a slow envelope at half the difference (C − D)/2. That is the sum-to-product formula, and it is why it turns "sum = 0" into "one factor = 0".

4 · Angles in AP: a chain of equal arrows beyond NCERT

Lay unit arrows at angles a, a + d, a + 2d, … tip to tail. The total sideways reach is Σ cos(a + kd) and the total height is Σ sin(a + kd). Each arrow turns by the same d, so the chain bends round a circle, like the sides of a regular polygon. When n arrows make a whole number of turns (nd a multiple of 360°), the chain closes and both sums are exactly 0.

Compound-angle, double-angle and sum-to-product formulas: NCERT Mathematics Class 11, Chapter 3. The stacked-triangle figure is the standard geometric proof for acute angles (as in S. L. Loney, Plane Trigonometry); the general case is in NCERT. The results a sin x + b cos x = √(a² + b²) sin(x + φ) and the sum Σ cos(a + kd) = [sin(nd/2) / sin(d/2)] cos(a + (n − 1)d/2) are beyond the NCERT text but standard for JEE; both are checked numerically in this page's script (the readouts show the direct sum next to the formula).
