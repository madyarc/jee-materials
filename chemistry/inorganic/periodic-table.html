Smaller atom, higher Zeff gives

IE ↑ (harder to remove e⁻)
ΔegH more negative (gains e⁻ more readily)
EN ↑ (pulls bonding e⁻ harder)

Where size fails

1. Subshell energy & stability (s vs p, half-filled, filled)
2. e⁻–e⁻ repulsion in very compact shells (2p)
3. Poor shielding by d and f electrons

Exceptions

Data: covalent radii from Cordero et al., Dalton Trans. 2008 (Mn, Fe, Co use low-spin values). First ionization enthalpies from NIST. ΔegH = −(electron affinity), electron affinities from the CRC Handbook; "> 0" means the anion is not stable (no bound state), the Po value (marked ≈) is calculated, not measured. ΔegH is not shown for d-block elements (not needed for JEE and many values are poorly known). Electronegativity: Pauling scale (CRC); Pb value is for Pb(IV). Zeff is computed live from Slater's rules for the outermost electron, which is a crude approximation. Lanthanides (58–71) and period 7 are omitted. Noble-gas covalent radii are not comparable to the van der Waals radii in NCERT tables, which is why NCERT shows noble gases as the largest in their period.
