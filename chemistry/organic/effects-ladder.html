Electronic Effects Ladder

Every "order" in organic chemistry comes from four effects pushing or pulling electron density. Instead of memorising lists, this page uses measured numbers (Hammett σ constants, pKa values, heats of hydrogenation) so you can see each effect's size and where it wins or loses.

The four effects

I Inductive

What moves
σ-bond electrons are polarised toward the more electronegative atom (permanent).
Range
Fades fast with each bond; small beyond about 3 bonds (see ladder 3).
Needs
Just a σ-bond path.
Signs
−I pulls (halogens, NO₂, CN, N⁺R₃). +I pushes (alkyl, O⁻, COO⁻).

R / M Resonance

What moves
π electrons and lone pairs delocalise through overlapping p orbitals.
Range
Carried along a conjugated chain without fading per bond; acts only at positions linked by conjugation (ortho/para on benzene, not meta).
Needs
Conjugation and roughly coplanar p orbitals.
Signs
+R donates a lone pair (NH₂, OH, OR, halogens). −R accepts into a π* (NO₂, CN, C=O).

H Hyperconjugation

What moves
Electrons from a C–H σ bond next door spill into an empty p orbital or π*.
Range
Only from the adjacent (α) carbon.
Needs
α C–H bonds. More α-H, more stabilisation (count them).
Shows up in
Carbocation, radical and alkene stability.

S Steric

What it is
Not electronic. Bulky groups crowd each other or block an approach.
Effects
Raises energy (cis vs trans alkenes), twists groups out of conjugation (switches off R), blocks attack (SN2 at 3° carbon).

Solv Solvation

What it is
Not an electronic effect either. In water, ions are stabilised by H-bonding to solvent.
Why it's here
It breaks some "pure +I/−I" predictions in water (see ladder 7).
Priority rule: resonance > hyperconjugation > inductive rule of thumb, not a law

It holds when the groups compared act at the same position. The classic failure is halogens on benzene: −I outweighs +R for overall reactivity (Cl: σp = +0.23, net withdrawing), yet the +R lone pair still decides where an electrophile attacks (ortho/para). Check Cl on the σ map below.

Substituent σ map: separating I from R

Hammett σ measures how much a substituent on a benzene ring raises the acidity of benzoic acid: σ = pKa(benzoic acid) − pKa(substituted). Positive means withdrawing, negative means donating. At meta the group acts mostly by induction. At para it acts by induction plus resonance. So the gap σp − σm gives a rough read of the resonance part approximate split.
